\section*{Preface}

{\fontsize{8.5}{12}\selectfont
This book, titled \emph{Preparation Course for Python} (PCP), is about learning Python with a clear destination in mind: the Fourier transform. It introduces the programming tools needed for scientific computing and applies them to a connected sequence of mathematical and signal-processing topics. Step by step, the reader moves from Python and NumPy basics through functions and visualization to complex numbers, exponential functions, signals, sampling, the discrete Fourier transform~(DFT), and finally the fast Fourier transform~(FFT).

This progression provides the guiding thread of the course. Fourier analysis is one of the central tools in science and engineering: it reveals the frequencies contained in a signal and supports numerous methods in signal processing, communications, and data analysis. Understanding the DFT and FFT requires several ideas to come together. Complex numbers provide the natural representation, the exponential function describes oscillations, sampling connects continuous and digital signals, and Python turns these concepts into executable algorithms. The FFT may appear rather mysterious when encountered for the first time. Our aim is to make it feel like the natural conclusion of the journey.

PCP is intended for students in engineering, computer science, and related fields who often bring complementary backgrounds. Some readers may know the mathematics but hesitate when faced with an empty code cell. Others may write Python confidently but feel less at home among complex exponentials and Fourier coefficients. PCP brings these perspectives together through concise explanations, executable examples, visualizations, sound examples, and exercises. No advanced programming knowledge is required; curiosity and a willingness to experiment are more important.

The material grew out of our teaching at the International Audio Laboratories Erlangen, a joint institution of the Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg~(FAU) and the Fraunhofer Institute for Integrated Circuits IIS. In particular, we use PCP as the basis for programming and laboratory courses for students in international Master's programs in computer science, artificial intelligence, data science, and electrical engineering. Its successor, the \emph{Preparation Course for PyTorch}~(PCPT)\footnote{\url{[https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/PCPT}}](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/PCPT}}), builds on PCP to introduce PyTorch, neural networks, and modern machine learning from a signal-processing perspective. PCP also prepares students for the \emph{Fundamentals of Music Processing}~(FMP)\footnote{\url{[https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/FMP}}](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/FMP}}) course, which uses Python to teach central methods for analyzing and processing music signals.

The accompanying Jupyter notebooks\footnote{\url{[https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/PCP}}](https://audiolabs-erlangen.de/PCP}}) combine text, equations, executable code, figures, exercises, and audio examples in an interactive environment. This book presents the same material as a coherent narrative. We recommend using both formats together: read a section in the book, try it in the notebook, change a parameter, and see or hear what happens. Unexpected results are not necessarily failures; they are often the most instructive part of an experiment. All examples use open-source software and run on a standard notebook computer.

The PCP material is organized into ten units, each opening with an orange box that gives a short overview and states the learning objectives. Explanations and executable code then alternate, so readers can move directly from understanding an idea to trying it out. Green boxes offer background information through brief excursions into mathematics, signal processing, applications, and other interesting facts. These optional detours add context and provide a welcome change of perspective without losing sight of the main path. Further orange boxes provide practical advice and draw attention to useful details or common pitfalls exactly where they arise. Each unit concludes with exercises in blue boxes, inviting readers to move from guided examples to independent problem solving. This visual structure makes it easy to recognize the main content, explore a rewarding side path, pause for advice, and put new knowledge into practice.

Beyond programming and mathematics, PCP introduces practices that are important for reproducible research, including reproducible software environments, documented functions, reusable modules, version control, and openly available code. The complete material is published under the MIT License and may be used for self-study or teaching.

Many students and colleagues have contributed their ideas, experience, and feedback to the PCP notebooks and this book. We are especially grateful to Sebastian Rosenzweig, who co-developed the original PCP notebooks and played a central role in shaping their early versions. We also thank, in alphabetical order, Vlora Arifi-Müller, Michael Krause, Heinrich Löllmann, Peter Meier, and Frank Zalkow for their valuable support. Questions and suggestions from generations of students have helped us refine the examples, improve the explanations, and better understand where difficulties and opportunities for insight arise.

We hope that PCP makes Python approachable, the mathematics tangible, and the FFT considerably less mysterious. Most importantly, we hope that readers enjoy exploring the connections between code, mathematics, and signals.
